# Dictionary correspondences and working tones

Updated 26 September 2026. Lotanna has confirmed all three correspondences below. Working tones are recorded separately from that confirmation; complete Ndebe spellings are not yet final. Start from a matching meaning and compare the forms; a list of synonyms alone does not prove a dialect correspondence. Tone marks below are source notation where transcribed, not a resolved modern Ndebe tone assignment.

## Working tone decisions

The D2 tone key, printed p.x / PDF p.13, explicitly defines unmarked high, macron step, and grave low. Its key and the full entry pages 38, 63 and 229 were rendered and visually checked. Text extraction corrupts some diacritics, especially underdots, so retain page references for later vowel-quality checks as well as tone checks.

| C01 | afa / aha | **áfà / áhà**, high–low | D1 explicitly marks both; D2 afà is high–low by its key. Source-supported, not an invented pattern. No claim that all dialects/contextual forms have identical tone. |
| C02 | mili / miri | **mílī**, high–step; provisional **mírī** for the corresponding lesson variant | D2 explicitly includes single-M milī. High–step is source-supported for that entry. The project’s shared-body choices are F/H/SH for the attested F/H correspondence, L/R for mili/miri, and N/L/Y for ani/ala/ana. This does not assert SH or Y variants for words where none have been supplied.

### Provisional-tone policy approved by Lotanna

Prefer candidates with clear meaning, sound correspondence and tone evidence. Where evidence is missing, an assumed tone is permitted for the working lesson. Label it **provisional assumption**, retain the source or reason, and replace it when better evidence arrives. Do not present an editorial assumption as dictionary evidence. A provisional example need not block the lesson build.

Lotanna confirms the orthographic mapping: **source step tone → Ndebe Mid tone**. Thus the source-supported high–step pattern for mili is rendered with Ndebe High–Mid forms. Keep “step” in the source evidence record and “Mid” in Ndebe instructions. The mapping is confirmed; transferring the word’s tone pattern to an unchecked dialect variant can still be provisional.

Correspondence approval and tone approval are separate: all three correspondences are creator-confirmed; source-supported tone readings apply to the entries cited, and transferred readings remain provisional. Dialect names remain unknown unless a source identifies them.

### Useful boundary case, not yet a lesson example

D2 printed p.10 / PDF p.44 pairs àgbọọ̀ and àgbọghọ̀ for a young girl. This involves the presence/absence of GH, not merely substitution between the sounds named on a shared body. Keep it as a future question about sequence representation, vowel length/hiatus and elision; do not assume the nzobe rule applies just because one form has fewer consonants.

## Nzobe and vowel correction confirmed — 26 September 2026

Lotanna confirms **nà + ànị̀ → n’ànị̀**. The surviving second a takes the elision form to show it hides the first a, which was swallowed. The final vowel in the headword is dot-below **ị**, with low tone: U+E258, not undotted low i U+E264. Both the Earth card and expanded/contracted nzobe sequences are corrected. This supersedes earlier notes calling the nzobe application unconfirmed or retaining undotted ani in project examples; historical source transcriptions remain evidence records.
